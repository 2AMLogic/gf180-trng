# DR-0014: Gate `sampler_dff`'s asynchronous reset into the storage loops' own inverters, rather than overriding either storage node with pull devices

## Status

- 2026-08-02: Accepted (Builder, #53).

## Context

### The cost that forced the choice

`sampler_dff` originally reset by brute force: an NMOS pulling the master's
storage node `m` to `vss` and a PMOS pulling the slave's storage node `s` to
`vdd`, both active whenever `rst_n = 0`, regardless of clock phase. Reset
takes, and the sizing is what makes it take — but the master's input
transmission gate is transparent whenever `clk = 0`, so while `rst_n = 0` **and**
`clk = 0` there is a resistive path from whatever drives `D`, through that gate,
into the reset pull-down. That is deliberate contention between two
simultaneously-ON pass devices, not leakage: a DC path that conducts for the
whole time reset is held with `clk` low.

[#48](https://github.com/2AMLogic/gf180-trng/issues/48) measured it rather than
fixing it, at both instances `sampler_core` contains and across the full
45-point PVT grid
(`sim/records/2026-08-01-sampler-dff-reset-current-{xsv,xsb}-{01..45}.md`).
The result: **967 µW at `tt`/27 °C/3.30 V and 1.71 mW at `ff`/−40 °C/3.63 V**
for the two instances together — 1.9× to 3.4× the whole block's ratified
`< 500 µW` active-power row ([`DR-0010`](DR-0010-raw-rate-moves-to-the-measured-jitter-energy-limit.md) §3),
on top of the entropy source's own 415 µW, and over that row at *every* one of
the 90 points measured. A single instance alone exceeded the entire row at the
grid minimum.

### Why "just park the clock" is not available

How long reset is asserted, and at what clock phase, is a power-on sequencing
question [#26](https://github.com/2AMLogic/gf180-trng/issues/26) owns and which
nothing in this repository has specified. The contention above only exists in
the `clk = 0` phase, so a system that guaranteed `clk` high (or stopped) for the
whole reset assertion would not pay it — but no such guarantee exists, and
[`DR-0012`](DR-0012-sampler-fixed-external-clock.md) makes `clk` a *fixed
external* clock supplied by the integrator, i.e. precisely the signal this
repository does not control. A sampler whose reset behaviour depends on the
integrator parking a clock it was never told to park is not a defined raw tap.

### What the cell must keep

`sampler_dff` is the only cell in this block that turns an analog swing into a
logic level, so its storage loops' regenerative behaviour is load-bearing:
[`DR-0012`](DR-0012-sampler-fixed-external-clock.md) §4 discharges DR-0003's
sustained-rate obligation *architecturally*, on the strength of the sampler
resolving correctly at every corner. Any change to a loop inverter changes that
loop's gain, so the setup/hold/metastability characterization has to be re-run,
not assumed to carry over.

## Decision

**We will gate `rst_n` into one inverter of each latch's hold loop — the
master's *forward* inverter (`m → mb`) and the slave's *feedback* inverter
(`q → qb`) — as 2-input NAND2s, and delete the reset pull devices on both
storage nodes.**

Concretely, in `design/xschem/sampler_dff.sch` (and therefore in the exported
`design/sampler_core.spice`, regenerated by `design/netlist.py`, never hand-edited):

1. `Mimp`/`Mimn` (the master's `m → mb` inverter) become
   `Mimpa`/`Mimpb`/`Mimna`/`Mimnb`: `mb = NAND(m, rst_n)`.
2. `Mis2p`/`Mis2n` (the slave's `q → qb` inverter) become
   `Mis2pa`/`Mis2pb`/`Mis2na`/`Mis2nb`: `qb = NAND(q, rst_n)`.
3. `Mrm` (the `m` pull-down) and `Mrs` (the `s` pull-up) are **deleted**, and
   with them the local `rst_n` inverter `Mpr`/`Mnr` and its internal `rst` node
   — a PMOS gated by `rst_n` is ON exactly when reset is asserted, so neither
   NAND2 needs the active-high copy, and nothing else in the cell consumed it.
4. Each NAND2's series NMOS leg is sized 2× the device it replaces (0.44 µm
   against a plain inverter's 0.22 µm) so the series stack's resistance matches
   the single device, matching the convention `xor2.sch` already uses. The
   parallel PMOS legs keep the original 0.44 µm. The loops' drive, and hence
   their regenerative gain, is therefore unchanged by construction as well as by
   measurement (§Consequences). Device count is unchanged at 22.

While `rst_n` is low both NAND2s are forced high, so `mb = 1` and `qb = 1` —
and those are the **only** two signals that can drive the slave's storage node
`s` (`mb` through `TG_S` while `clk = 1`, `qb` through `TG_FBS` while
`clk = 0`). They agree, so `s = 1` and `Q = 0` in either clock phase and across
every clock edge; node `m` is driven to 0 through the master's own feedback path
(`mb = 1 → INVM2 → mc = 0 → TG_FBM`) on every `clk = 1` phase. No reset device
shares a node with a transmission gate, so the contention path does not exist to
be sized against.

**The asymmetry is the decision.** The master is gated at its *forward*
inverter and the slave at its *feedback* inverter, and these are not
interchangeable — see Alternatives.

## Alternatives considered

### Gate the *feedback* inverter of each latch (`INVM2` on the master, `INVS2` on the slave)

- **What**: the arrangement #53 was filed proposing — leave `m → mb` a plain
  inverter and make `mb → mc` the master's NAND2, so reset reaches `m` through
  the same feedback path.
- **Why plausible**: it is the symmetric reading of "gate reset into the loop",
  it touches only the two inverters that feed nothing outside their own loop,
  and it holds `Q = 0` correctly in **both static clock phases** — so a
  DC-biased testbench, or a reading of the schematic that stops at the static
  cases, cannot tell it apart from the arrangement chosen.
- **Why rejected**: it leaves `mb` tracking `D` while reset is asserted, and
  `mb` is exactly what `TG_S` hands the slave on each `clk` **rising** edge.
  With `D = 1` that drives `s = 0` and hence `Q = 1` for the whole `clk`-high
  phase, every phase. Measured on `sim/tb/sampler-dff-reset-clocked/` (a scratch
  `--no-write` run against a hand-modified netlist, since this variant is not a
  cell this repository ships): `Q` reaches **3.05 V / 3.53 V / 3.72 V** at
  `ss`/125 °C/2.97 V, `tt`/27 °C/3.30 V and `ff`/−40 °C/3.63 V — a full rail, at
  every corner, and `Q` is still at the full supply at the instant `rst_n`
  releases. Reset is not glitching under this variant; it is **defeated**
  whenever `clk` is high. The chosen arrangement reads 116 / 134 / 149 mV at the
  same three points (~4 % of supply, clock coupling through the transmission
  gates), and the pre-#53 brute-force cell reads 127 / 147 / 163 mV. This is the
  specific failure the "gate reset into the loops" instruction is easy to walk
  into, and it is why this record exists rather than a commit message.

### Gate the slave's *forward* inverter (`s → q`) as well, for symmetry with the master

- **What**: make the slave's `s → q` inverter the gated one, mirroring the
  master exactly.
- **Why plausible**: an identical master and slave is easier to reason about and
  easier to lay out as one repeated cell.
- **Why rejected**: two independent reasons. Polarity — `Q` must reset to 0, so
  forcing the *output* of `s → q` requires a NOR gated by an active-high `rst`,
  which reintroduces the local reset inverter this decision deletes. And
  placement — `s → q` is the `clk`→`Q` path itself, so a series stack there
  directly costs propagation delay on the one path
  [`DR-0012`](DR-0012-sampler-fixed-external-clock.md) §4's rate argument leans
  on. The chosen arrangement puts both NAND2s on `mb` and `qb`, neither of which
  is on that path.

### Keep the brute-force reset and gate the master's input transmission gate off during reset

- **What**: leave `Mrm`/`Mrs` in place and add `rst_n` to `TG_D`'s control, so
  the input gate is opaque while reset is asserted and there is nothing for the
  pull-down to fight.
- **Why plausible**: it targets the contention path directly and leaves the
  storage loops — and therefore the metastability characterization — completely
  untouched, which is the cheapest possible change to re-verify.
- **Why rejected**: it puts reset logic in the sampler's **aperture**. `TG_D`'s
  control is the `clk`/`clkb` pair that defines when `D` is captured, and adding
  a gate to it adds delay and skew to exactly the edge whose setup/hold window
  DR-0012 §4 depends on — trading a power problem for a timing problem in the
  one place this block cannot afford one. It also keeps two oversized pull
  devices hanging on the storage nodes, whose capacitance the shipped
  arrangement removes (and whose removal is why `clk`→`Q` got *faster*; see
  §Consequences).

### Do nothing: keep the brute-force reset and specify a clock/reset relationship in #26

- **What**: accept the contention and require, as a system-level constraint,
  that `clk` be parked high (or stopped) for the whole reset assertion.
- **Why plausible**: no schematic change, no re-characterization, and the
  contention genuinely does not occur in the `clk`-high phase.
- **Why rejected**: it converts a fixed, internal property of the cell into an
  integration constraint on a clock this repository explicitly does not control
  ([`DR-0012`](DR-0012-sampler-fixed-external-clock.md)), and it would have to
  be honoured by every integrator forever, silently, on pain of a 3.4×
  power-budget overrun that produces no functional symptom. #26 has enough real
  system-level obligations without inheriting one this cell can simply not have.

## Consequences

- **Positive**:
  - The reset window's supply draw falls from **967 µW to 66.3 nW** at nominal
    and from **1.71 mW to 119 nW** at the two grids' worst points — a factor of
    ≈14,600 and ≈14,400 respectively, taking the reset window from 3.4× the
    ratified `< 500 µW` row to **0.024 %** of it. The same two testbenches,
    unchanged, on two cells: `sim/records/2026-08-02-sampler-dff-reset-current-{xsv,xsb}-{01..45}.md`
    against #48's `2026-08-01-…` set. The residual is ordinary off-device
    leakage, not a reduced contention path — there is no longer a DC path from
    `D` to `vss` to reduce.
  - **No metastable hang at any of the 45 PVT points**, re-measured on the new
    cell (`sim/records/2026-08-02-sampler-dff-setup-hold-{01..45}.md`). The
    property [`DR-0012`](DR-0012-sampler-fixed-external-clock.md) §4's rate
    argument rests on survives the change to the loops.
  - `clk`→`Q` got **faster**, 71.4–170.4 ps rising against 82.3–194.3 ps before,
    and the setup-time bracket moved down with it (the 59 ps margin is captured
    at 20 of 45 points against 13 of 45). This is a side effect of deleting an
    0.88 µm PMOS from node `s` and an 0.44 µm NMOS from node `m`, not of the
    NAND2s, and it is a consequence rather than a goal.
  - Reset is now demonstrably clock-phase-independent with the clock
    **running**, not merely at DC: `sim/tb/sampler-dff-reset-clocked/`, 45-point
    grid (`sim/records/2026-08-01-sampler-dff-reset-clocked-{01..45}.md`), `Q`
    departs from its reset value by at most 4.22 % of supply and returns to
    ≤ 4.03 µV before release. That testbench did not exist before this decision;
    it exists because a gated reset moves the burden of proof onto the clock
    edge, where neither of the two DC reset-current decks nor
    `sim/tb/sampler-dff-setup-hold/` can see.
  - The functional raw-bitstream behaviour is unchanged:
    `sim/records/2026-08-01-sampler-array-digitize-{03,04}.md` against the
    pre-#53 `{01,02}` — same `raw_bit`/`raw_valid` handshake, `raw_valid` high
    from the first edge after release, every bit within 20 µV of a rail, ring
    frequencies and `xo` swing within 0.1 %.

- **Negative / accepted cost**:
  - The two latches are no longer structurally identical, and the asymmetry is
    not self-evident from the netlist — gating the "obvious" symmetric pair
    silently defeats reset (see Alternatives). That is a standing trap for
    anyone who later "tidies" this cell, which is why the schematic header
    states it inline and this record exists.
  - Each hold loop now contains a series NMOS stack rather than a single device.
    The 2× sizing compensates for it and the measured setup/hold and
    metastability behaviour is unchanged-or-better, but a future re-sizing of
    this cell inherits a two-transistor stack to reason about rather than one
    device.
  - The `sim/tb/sampler-dff-reset-current-{xsv,xsb}/` decks now measure leakage
    at a bias where they used to measure contention. They are kept unchanged on
    purpose — so the before/after comparison is one measurement on two cells —
    but their names and headers have to carry that history, and a reader who
    cites one of the new records for "the reset contention current" would be
    citing a number for a mechanism that no longer exists. Each record's
    `netlist.sha` says which cell it ran against; the caveats say what the
    number means for each.
  - The one bitstream difference at `tt` in `sampler-array-digitize` (`b1`
    flips) is real and is not a functional regression: at that deck's 10 ns
    clock the bitstream is a deterministic function of ring phase at the
    sampling instants, and the change moved the sampler's input-node parasitics
    slightly. It does mean the two digitize record sets are not bit-comparable,
    only behaviour-comparable.

- **Follow-up required**:
  - None for [`DR-0011`](DR-0011-metastability-hybrid-tap-claims-and-scope.md).
    #53's acceptance criteria asked whether the changed loop behaviour affects
    that record's sizing argument: **it does not**, and the reason is structural
    rather than a judgement call. DR-0011 is about the metastability-hybrid
    stretch tap — `meta_inv`, `meta_nand2`, `meta_arb`, `ro_meta_tap`,
    `ro_array_core_meta` — and its regeneration-time bound (§2) and trim-drift
    figure (§3) are measured on `meta_arb`, a cross-coupled NAND2 SR latch in a
    separate schematic that this change does not touch and that never
    instantiates `sampler_dff`. No sizing argument in DR-0011 refers to
    `sampler_dff`, and no record DR-0011 cites was produced by a testbench that
    instantiates it.
  - [`DR-0012`](DR-0012-sampler-fixed-external-clock.md)'s "Evidence as of
    2026-08-01" bullet names the pre-#53 record set. It is left as written (it
    was true of the cell that existed then); this same change appends a dated
    bullet pointing at the re-characterization, so the DR names the evidence for
    the cell that actually ships.
  - `sim/tb/sampler-dff-reset-clocked/` covers reset release *between* clock
    edges only. Releasing `rst_n` coincident with a `clk` edge is a distinct
    question no testbench in this repository probes, and #26 should say whether
    the integration contract needs it answered.

- **Revisit if**: #26 fixes a power-on sequencing contract that would have made
  the brute-force reset's `clk`-low window unreachable anyway (which would not
  restore the pull devices, but would change what this record's power argument
  was worth); or a layout pass finds the series stacks cost enough area or
  parasitic capacitance to reopen the sizing in §Decision item 4; or a future
  sampler re-targeting moves the clock frequency far enough that the ~4 %
  clock-coupling excursion measured on `sim/tb/sampler-dff-reset-clocked/` stops
  being negligible against the aperture.
